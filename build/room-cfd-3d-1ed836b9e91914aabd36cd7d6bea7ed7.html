Room CFD 3D

An office, by default 5 m deep, 4 m wide and 2.5 m high, cooled by outdoor air through a window in the facade. The air leaves through an exhaust high in the back wall. The whole room is computed in 3D on the graphics card, so a window narrower than the room can be studied.

About the model

The solver is room_cfd_3d.py, ported to WebGPU compute shaders: the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with Boussinesq buoyancy and an energy equation, on a staggered grid, marching in time with a pressure projection. The pressure equation is solved by conjugate gradients with a multigrid preconditioner. Turbulence: standard k-ε with wall functions (default), Chen & Xu (1998), or a constant νt of 0.002 m²/s. The graphics card computes in single precision; on a test case it agrees with the Python version to about 1 part in a million.

The heat gains sit in a box from the floor up, by default 0.6 m deep, 0.8 m wide and 1.2 m high in the middle of the room; move and resize it under Heat source or click in the plan. The occupied zone starts by default 0.6 m from the facade, back and side walls and reaches 1.8 m up; change it under Occupied zone. The readouts are at ankle height (0.1 m). ISO 7730 DR uses Tu = 40 %; the ASHRAE 55 ankle draught model uses the PMV of the occupied zone at 1.2 met, 0.5 clo, 50 % RH and tr = ta. Both limits are 20 %.

A layout replaces the single heat source with any number of heat sources, heated floor patches and furniture, read from a text file in the same format as read_layout in room_cfd_3d.py. The heat gains are then the sum of the powers in the file, and the room temperature follows from them. Furniture is solid and adiabatic: no air or heat passes through it, and its surfaces are no-slip walls. A box takes the cells whose centres lie inside it (grey in the plots). A thin plate sits on the cell faces nearest to it, so it blocks the flow without taking up cells. The k-ε wall functions are applied only at the room walls, not on the furniture.

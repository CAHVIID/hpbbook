# Appendix: Air-conditioning in Europe, summer 2026

:::{warning} Under revision
This appendix is being reworked and may change substantially.
:::

The heat waves of June and July 2026 showed how air-conditioning (AC) shapes electricity demand in Europe. Solar PV covered much of the midday cooling, but AC kept running after sunset, and the grid strain and price spikes came in the early evening.

## Demand during the heat waves

Daily electricity demand rose mainly because of AC use {cite:p}`ember2026heat`:

| Country | Daily demand increase | Peak demand increase | Daily consumption, 11 → 25 June (GWh) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| France | up to 14% | 9% | 1,048 → 1,255 |
| Spain | up to 13% | 11% | – |
| Germany | – | – | 1,267 → 1,396 |
| Italy | up to 28% | 14% | – |

Increases are relative to 13–19 June; daily consumption from {cite:t}`euronews2026cooling`. No demand figures were found for the UK. In France, RTE puts the effect at 0.7 to 1 GW per °C of outdoor temperature, depending on the time of day {cite:p}`euronews2026cooling`.

## Timing: midday versus evening

On heat-wave days, EU solar output was up to 17% higher than on other days, and solar supplied 25% of EU electricity in June and July {cite:p}`ember2026heat`. According to the IEA, cooling demand is highest during the day {cite:p}`euronews2026cooling`. Demand stays high into the early evening, however, after solar output has fallen. Day-ahead prices spiked from around 19:00, reaching €313/MWh in France on 24 June {cite:p}`ember2026heat`. A German study expects the rise in consumption from AC to come around 19:00 {cite:p}`gem2026heat`.

## AC ownership

| Country | Households with AC |
| --- | --- |
| Italy | nearly 60% |
| Spain | 41% |
| France | 25% (at least 60% on the Mediterranean, often below 5% in the north, including Paris) |
| Germany | 3–6% |
| UK | below 5% |
| EU | about 20% |

Figures compiled by {cite:t}`carbonbriefac` from national sources of different years, and EU figure from the IEA {cite:p}`euronews2026cooling`. Ownership in the EU has risen by about half in ten years {cite:p}`euronews2026cooling`.

## Consequence for design

Northern Europe still has little AC, but sales are growing. Every AC unit added to a home that could have been kept cool passively adds to the evening peak, when solar output is low and gas plants set the price. This is the case for the cooling strategy in [](d2-cooling-strategy.md), where AC is the last step.

## Introduction

The glazing is most of the window area, and its properties decide how much heat is lost, how much solar heat comes in and how much daylight reaches the room. Modern glazing units are sealed units of two or three panes with coatings and a gas fill between them, and their properties can be tuned for low heat loss, high solar gain, solar control or daylight.

This chapter covers the three numbers on every glazing datasheet and the physics behind them.

## The three key properties

- **U-value, $U_g$** (W/(m²K)): the heat loss through the centre of the glazing, calculated by EN 673 {cite:p}`en673`.
- **Total solar energy transmittance, $g$** (–): the fraction of the incident solar radiation that ends up as heat in the room, both transmitted directly and absorbed in the panes and released inwards {cite:p}`en410`.
- **Light transmittance, $\tau_v$** (–): the fraction of visible light that passes through {cite:p}`en410`.

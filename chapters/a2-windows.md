## Introduction

A window is the part of the envelope that has to do the most: let in daylight and solar heat, give a view, open for venting, keep out rain and noise, and still lose as little heat as possible. Even the best window loses several times more heat per square metre than the wall around it, so the window area, the window itself and the way it is fitted into the wall have a large effect on the heat loss of a low-energy building.

This chapter treats the window as a whole: frame, glazing and the edge where they meet. The glazing itself is the subject of the next chapter.

## The U-value of a window

The window U-value combines the glazing, the frame and the linear thermal bridge at the edge of the glazing, where the spacer holds the panes apart {cite:p}`iso10077`:

```{math}
:label: eq-win-uw
U_w = \frac{A_g U_g + A_f U_f + l_g \, \psi_g}{A_g + A_f}
```

where $A_g$ and $A_f$ are the visible glazing area and the frame area (m²), $U_g$ and $U_f$ their U-values (W/(m²K)), $l_g$ the visible perimeter of the glazing (m) and $\psi_g$ the linear thermal transmittance of the glazing edge (W/(m·K)).

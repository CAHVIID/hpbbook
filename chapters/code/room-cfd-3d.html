Room CFD 3D

A 5 m deep, 4 m wide and 2.5 m high office cooled by outdoor air through a window in the facade. The air leaves through an exhaust high in the back wall. The whole room is computed in 3D on the graphics card, so a window narrower than the room can be studied.

About the model

The solver is room_cfd_3d.py from the course, ported to WebGPU compute shaders: the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with Boussinesq buoyancy and an energy equation, on a staggered grid, marching in time with a pressure projection. The pressure equation is solved by conjugate gradients with a multigrid preconditioner. Turbulence: standard k-ε with wall functions (default), Chen & Xu (1998), or a constant νt of 0.002 m²/s. The graphics card computes in single precision; on a test case it agrees with the Python version to about 1 part in a million.

The heat gains sit in a 0.4 m deep block across the room at x = 2.3–2.7 m, up to 1.2 m. The occupied zone starts 0.6 m from the facade, back and side walls. The readouts are at ankle height (0.1 m). ISO 7730 DR uses Tu = 40 %; the ASHRAE 55 ankle draught model uses the PMV of the occupied zone at 1.2 met, 0.5 clo, 50 % RH and tr = ta. Both limits are 20 %.
